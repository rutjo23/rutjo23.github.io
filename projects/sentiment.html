10-K Sentiment Analysis

Research question

How can the tone of a long annual report be summarized in a way that allows comparison across filings? This project converts the language in corporate 10-K reports into numerical measures of positive, negative, and litigious wording.

Approach

The analysis removes HTML formatting, separates the remaining text into words, and counts matches against the Loughran–McDonald financial dictionary. A dictionary developed for financial disclosures helps distinguish financial usage from everyday language.

For each category, the number of matching words is divided by the total word count. These proportions make reports of different lengths easier to compare than raw word counts alone.

Results

The saved analysis produced a dataset with 100 rows and 8 columns and a CSV export. It turns unstructured filing text into document-level sentiment measures that can support comparisons of financial tone.

Interpretation

The measures describe word usage rather than the meaning of every sentence. Dictionary matching does not directly account for negation or context, so a higher negative-word share should not by itself be interpreted as worse company performance.
