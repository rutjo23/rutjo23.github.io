01ASSIGNMENTS 3–4

10-K Sentiment Analysis

Financial tone in annual reports

View project & resultsClose details+

Research question

How can the tone of a long annual report be summarized in a way that allows comparison across filings? This project converts the language in corporate 10-K reports into numerical measures of positive, negative, and litigious wording.

Approach

The analysis removes HTML formatting, separates the remaining text into words, and counts matches against the Loughran–McDonald financial dictionary. A dictionary developed for financial disclosures helps distinguish financial usage from everyday language.

For each category, the number of matching words is divided by the total word count. These proportions make reports of different lengths easier to compare than raw word counts alone.

Results

The saved analysis produced a dataset with 100 rows and 8 columns and a CSV export. It turns unstructured filing text into document-level sentiment measures that can support comparisons of financial tone.

Interpretation

The measures describe word usage rather than the meaning of every sentence. Dictionary matching does not directly account for negation or context, so a higher negative-word share should not by itself be interpreted as worse company performance.

02ASSIGNMENT 5

Readability & Forward-Looking Language

Complexity and future-oriented disclosure

View project & resultsClose details+

Research question

How difficult are financial disclosures to read, and does language about the future differ from the document as a whole? This project examines both linguistic complexity and the wording companies use to discuss expectations.

Approach

The analysis separates text into sentences and identifies potentially forward-looking sentences using modal words and related expressions. It then measures words, sentences, and complex words for the full text and the selected sentences.

Syllable estimates help identify complex vocabulary. The Gunning FOG index combines average sentence length with the share of complex words: longer sentences and more complex vocabulary increase the score.

Results

The project developed feature-extraction functions and saved illustrative examples for readability and forward-looking language. These provide a way to compare the complexity of an entire disclosure with the complexity of its future-oriented statements.

Interpretation

The saved work demonstrates the measurement workflow; it does not establish an aggregate finding across filings. Language rules can also select sentences expressing uncertainty or obligation, and readability scores describe textual complexity rather than directly measuring reader understanding.

03ASSIGNMENT 6

MD&A Extraction & Disclosure Analytics

Management commentary and section-level analysis

View project & resultsClose details+

Research question

How does management discuss business performance, and how does that discussion compare with the rest of an annual report? This project focuses on Management’s Discussion and Analysis (MD&A), where management explains operating results and financial conditions.

Approach

The workflow checks filing types, cleans the filing text, and searches for Item 7, the MD&A section. It looks for an ending boundary at Item 7A or Item 8 and selects the longest candidate section to reduce confusion with shorter references such as table-of-contents entries.

The same sentiment, readability, and future-oriented language measures are calculated for the complete filing and the extracted section. Applying a common set of measures allows comparisons between management commentary and the wider disclosure.

Results

The exported dataset contains 101 rows and 59 columns. Automated checks marked 89 records valid and flagged 12 as not annual reports. The saved notebook also records a 61-column version containing the full filing and extracted MD&A text.

Interpretation

The result is a structured dataset for section-level disclosure analysis, rather than evidence of a particular economic relationship. Section boundaries vary across filings, so automated validity flags and extracted passages require manual review before drawing conclusions.

04ASSIGNMENTS 7–8

Remarks Lab

Interpretable earnings-call classification

View project & resultsClose details+

Research question

Can word patterns distinguish prepared management remarks from answers during earnings-call Q&A? Remarks Lab explores the difference between a planned presentation and management responses to analyst questions.

Approach

The project uses call and speaker-turn data to identify candidate management answers following question cues. It trains a multinomial Naive Bayes classifier, which combines evidence from word frequencies to assign each sentence to one of the two categories.

Companies are separated into training and test groups so evaluation uses companies excluded from training. The model uses equal class priors and smoothing to handle words with few or no observations in a class.

Results

Training used 44,907 sentences from 159 companies. Evaluation used 10,954 sentences from 40 held-out companies. The model achieved 90.48% agreement with the provisional automatic labels and 90.46% balanced accuracy, which gives equal weight to recall for both categories.

Interactive experience

The browser demo displays the predicted category, both class scores, normalized probabilities, and individual word contributions. This makes it possible to inspect which words push a sentence toward prepared remarks or Q&A.

Interpretation

These scores measure agreement with automatically assigned labels rather than independently verified human judgments. The displayed probabilities are uncalibrated and should be read as model outputs, not guaranteed confidence levels.
